# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | We will become the source of truth for all projects that all field users turn to when they need more information, or have updates to complete. |
| **Where to play** | We will build Foundations for foremen and field workers that need to communicate progress, upload proof of work, and check expectations against work agreements. |
| **How to win** | We have a robust platform that goes beyond field management, and helps contractors make informed business decisions to shape the growth of their business. |
| **Capabilities required** | We will provide a mobile experience that surfaces the most important functionality related to the jobs to be done in the field first, but allows users to get more depth or track overall progress when needed. |
| **Management systems** | We will track habit formation by measuring the growth of our daily active users, and track value provided by measuring growth in key metrics per user such as updates saved, photos and documents uploaded, and time tracked. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not focus on deeper analytics or more robust integrations, because those strategies are aimed at the web users that are already having success with our product instead of the field users that have not yet bought in.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Become the single system of record for all Meridian users while in the field.
>
> - **KR1:** 55% of field users activated this quarter are active three or more days a week at week 8.
> - **KR2:** 60% of field updates get an office action (view, assign, close, or link to an RFI) within 24 hours.
> - **KR3:** Cut median days from work complete to approval by 35% on items with Meridian field updates.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** The AI challenged some of my initial metrics in my OKRs, with the goal to have them check against repeated usage and value. The initial versions would not have shown whether we had actually created new habits with field users.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** I've updated my OKRs to be more specific around repeated usage and habit formation.
